# Rubric: how a hiring manager reads the original CV

Rate the ORIGINAL CV, never the suggested one. Use 1 (weak), 2 (adequate), 3 (strong), or "unassessed" when the material cannot be read. No weights, totals or percentages. The verdict follows from the ratings by the rule in `report-layout.md`.

Rate how well the document presents the career it describes. Rate fit for the target role separately, in the requirements table. A strong functional CV must not lose points because the target asks for broader responsibilities.

## The seven areas

| Area | 1 Weak | 2 Adequate | 3 Strong |
|---|---|---|---|
| Positioning and value | Generic opening; identity and contribution unclear | Function and strengths identifiable, but the argument is scattered or generic | Opening states a specific professional identity and credible contribution, backed up later in the CV |
| Scope and accountability | Titles and duties give little sense of scale or authority | Some scope stated; important boundaries unclear | Business scale, complexity and actual accountability are clear |
| Achievement evidence | Mostly duties or unsupported praise | Some specific outcomes; contribution or context incomplete | Main claims connect personal contribution to meaningful outcomes, with context and scale |
| Leadership or specialist contribution | Labels replace evidence of influence or judgment | Examples exist, but consequences are unclear | Shows consequential decisions, influence, capability building or specialist impact that fits the career path |
| Career story and relevance | Chronology or priorities obscure the experience | Progression understandable; important material buried or compressed | Coherent chronology; relevant experience is easy to find, including older relevant roles |
| Clarity and usability | Important information hard to find | Usable, with repetition or uneven hierarchy | Precise, economical, clear hierarchy, readable in 30 seconds |
| Skills language | No skills section, or only generic traits ("team player", "strategic thinker", "MS Office") | Some role-specific skills, but in in-house language, or not backed by evidence elsewhere in the CV | Named, market-standard capabilities, each backed by evidence in the experience section |

One metric does not earn a 3, and one missing metric does not mean a 1. If one weakness affects two areas, name each distinct consequence once instead of repeating the criticism.

## Calibration

- Leadership can be shown through consequential decisions, influence, capability building or specialist impact. These are alternative routes, not a checklist. Large teams, management layers and succession stories are not required. Never reward title or headcount alone.
- Adapt to the path:
  - **Functional leader:** remit, enterprise contribution, budget authority, stakeholder influence.
  - **GM or P&L leader:** the business boundaries they actually owned, decision rights, commercial outcomes.
  - **Senior specialist:** technical complexity, judgment, adoption, risk reduction. Direct reports are optional.
  - **Pivot:** transferable evidence and its limits. Adjacent experience is not equivalent experience.
  - **Portfolio or interim executive:** clients, dates, concurrent roles, advisory versus executive authority.
- Keep these distinctions: budget management versus P&L ownership, project support versus programme leadership, matrix influence versus direct reports, board exposure versus board membership.
- Accept confidentiality. Keep qualitative evidence and ranges the user supplied, and never estimate an undisclosed number.

## Target requirements (only when a target is supplied)

Record six to eight requirements; merge minor ones and leave out anything the level check already covers. For each: requirement, priority (Essential, Desirable or Stated, taken from the job description and never invented), whether it is core (what the job is fundamentally hiring for: usually two or three, marked `"core": true`), evidence status, where the evidence sits in the CV, and next action.

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Clearly demonstrated | Directly relevant evidence in the supplied CV (still unverified) |
| Partly demonstrated | Relevant evidence, but incomplete in scope, recency or outcome |
| Not demonstrated in supplied material | Not visible in the CV. The user may still have it |
| Confirmed gap | The user has explicitly said they do not have it |

Split a requirement whenever its parts have different evidence, for example total leadership years versus senior years in a named industry, or regional versus global remit. Do not award a match because a job description says something is "not required". For a short role paragraph instead of a full job description, say the comparison is broad.

Name up to three decisive unresolved requirements in `summary.needs_evidence`. Rank by what would actually decide the hire, not by keyword count.

## Level check (only when a target is supplied)

Compare the level the job description asks for (years, scope, team size, hands-on versus strategic) with the level the CV shows. Result: match, over, under or unclear. Over-level is as real a screening risk as under-level. State it neutrally and never advise hiding dates or experience.

## Keyword check (only when a target is supplied)

Pick six to ten terms a recruiter would search for from this job description: capabilities, domains and certifications, not soft traits. For each term, record whether it appears in the original CV and whether it appears in the suggested CV. Add a term to the suggested CV only when the CV's evidence supports it, and say why in `note`. Where you did not add a term, say so ("Not added: no evidence in your CV"). This visible restraint is part of the product.

## Health checks (always)

Run each check and mark it `ok`, `fix` (fixed in the suggested CV) or `flag` (the user must act):

- **Contact details:** phone, email and location (city is enough).
- **LinkedIn URL:** present and tidy. Never invent one.
- **Length:** original page count, and the suggested CV's length.
- **Dates:** one consistent format; month and year where the source gives them.
- **Chronology:** reverse chronological order; overlaps and gaps noted neutrally. Never advise hiding them.
- **Personal data a hiring manager does not need (Singapore and most markets):** photo, NRIC or FIN number, full date of birth or age, marital status, race, religion. Leave these out of the suggested CV and flag them so the user also removes them from other copies. An NRIC or FIN number is an identity number and should never be on a CV.
- **File and layout risks:** tables, text boxes, columns, headers holding key details, or images of text that can break applicant tracking systems.
- **Tone:** first-person "I" throughout, or unsupported superlatives ("world-class", "visionary").

Report six to eight checks, choosing the ones that matter most for this CV.
