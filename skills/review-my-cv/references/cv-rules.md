# Writing the suggested improved CV

## Shape and length

- Two pages is the target and three pages is the hard maximum. Recruiters and headhunters rarely read past page three, so a longer CV works against the candidate. Cut repetition, duties and old detail before cutting relevant evidence.
- Single column, reverse chronological order, the official job titles, no tables or graphics. No photo, NRIC or FIN number, date of birth, marital status, race or religion.
- Sections, in order: name, optional headline, contact line, Profile, Key skills (optional), Professional experience, Earlier career (optional), Education, Professional qualifications, and other sections only if the source supports them.
- No Career Agility International or CARLY™ branding, coaching notes, questions, placeholders or ratings anywhere in the CV.
- If no name is supplied, use a source-supported professional heading (for example "Supply Chain Director") and set `"name_is_heading": true`. Never invent a name or contact details.

## Recency and relevance

1. **Default (no target, or target matched by recent roles):** give full treatment (scope line plus three to five outcome bullets) to the two or three most recent roles, or those covering roughly the past six to eight years. Older roles go under "Earlier career", compressed to one line each (title, employer, dates) or one line plus one bullet. Keep every older role's title, employer and dates, so the chronology never looks like it hides a gap.
2. **With a target, relevance beats recency.** If an older role holds the strongest evidence for the target, do not compress it. Give it a full entry (scope plus two or three bullets) inside "Earlier career", and mention it in the opening profile, for example "Earlier, eight years leading regional procurement across six Asian markets." Recruiters skim the top of page one, so relevance buried on page two may never be read.
3. When rule 2 applies, the report must flag the recency gap honestly as the thing an interviewer will probe (`hiring_manager_read.interview_probe`).
4. Never re-order roles out of chronology or blur dates to make old experience look recent.

## Profile

Three to five sentences. Say who the candidate is (function, level, industry and scale), the two or three strongest pieces of evidence, and, if there is a target, the experience the target is buying. Every phrase must trace to the CV. Avoid "results-driven", "dynamic", "passionate", "proven track record" and similar filler.

## Bullets

- Lead with the outcome when there is one: "Cut finished-goods inventory from 52 to 38 days by introducing monthly S&OP."
- Keep every number exactly as given: the same unit and currency, percentage versus percentage points, and time period. Never add a currency, period or region the source did not give.
- Keep attribution. "Member of the team that..." stays a team claim. "Supported" never becomes "led". Contributing to another site's project never becomes leading it.
- A derived figure is allowed only when every input is explicit and the arithmetic is unambiguous. Otherwise leave it out.
- Prefer one strong bullet to three weak ones.

## Key skills (the CV's own list)

At most six skills, in plain market language. Each must be backed by evidence in the experience section. This is a tidy, credible list, not a taxonomy: the deeper skills mapping is what CARLY™ does. Do not pad it with job-description terms the CV cannot support.

## Facts the user adds in chat

If the user confirms a new fact (for example "I did lead the Malaysian rollout"), it may go into the CV and the evidence record. Treat CARLY™ output the same way: it needs explicit confirmation from the user before it becomes a CV fact.
